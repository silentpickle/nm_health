# Getting records from the City of Albuquerque (IPRA how-to)

Use this only for gaps: things the city does not publish, such as full inspection reports for weeks we
missed (the weekly Media Report PDF is overwritten every week), inspector photographs, or inspector
narrative. Not legal advice. Facts below were checked in November 2026; items marked
**(unverified)** could not be confirmed on a primary page.

Check first whether we already have it: `data/archive/` holds every weekly Media Report PDF we caught,
and the Info Connect feed already gives statuses and violation counts.

## 1. What IPRA gives you

IPRA is the Inspection of Public Records Act, NMSA 1978 sections 14-2-1 to 14-2-12. Statute text below
is from the NM Department of Justice (formerly the Attorney General's office) IPRA Compliance Guide,
Ninth Edition, which reprints the full act ([guide](https://nmdoj.gov/wp-content/uploads/NMDOJ-IPRA-Guide-Ninth-Edition-25.03.21.pdf)).
The guide is dated 2024 (file name suggests a March 2025 posting). I could not open the live statute on
a state site, so check for amendments since then **(unverified as current)**.

- **Anyone can ask.** Section 14-2-8(C): a written request must give your name, address and
  telephone number and identify the records "with reasonable particularity." Nobody can be required
  "to state the reason for inspecting the records." Do not explain why you want them.
- **Email counts as written.** Section 14-2-8(F).
- **Existing records only.** Section 14-2-8(B): the city does not have to create a record. Ask for
  documents that already exist.
- **Photographs are public records.** Section 14-2-6(H) lists "photographs, recordings" among public records.
- **Deadlines (section 14-2-8(D)):**
  - Permit inspection "immediately or as soon as is practicable," but no later than **15 calendar days**
    after the written request.
  - If not permitted within **3 business days**, the custodian must explain in writing when the
    records will be available or when the body will respond. The 3 days start only when the request
    reaches the custodian's office.
  - A request not fulfilled within 15 days "may be deemed denied" (section 14-2-11(A)).
- **"Excessively burdensome or broad" (section 14-2-10):** the city may take "an additional reasonable
  period of time," but must tell you in writing within 15 days that it needs more time. If the records
  still do not come in a reasonable time, you may treat the request as denied.
  Narrow requests are much less likely to be called burdensome.
- **Electronic copies (section 14-2-9(B)):** if the record exists electronically and you ask for an
  electronic copy, the city must give one, in the file format it already has.
- **Fees (section 14-2-9(C)):** reasonable copying fees, capped at $1.00 per printed page; actual cost of
  a disk or storage device and of mailing/emailing; advance payment allowed; no fee for the time spent
  deciding whether a record is disclosable.
- **Redactions (section 14-2-9(A)):** exempt parts must be separated out and the rest released.
- **Denials (section 14-2-11):** must be written, describe the records, name the people responsible, and
  arrive within 15 days. A custodian who misses that can owe damages of up to $100 per day.

## 2. City of Albuquerque specifics

- **Where to file:** the NextRequest portal, [cabq.nextrequest.com](https://cabq.nextrequest.com/)
  (the city's own guide calls it abqrecords.cabq.gov, which leads to the same form). Mail and in-person
  filing with the Office of the City Clerk also work ([City Clerk public records page](https://www.cabq.gov/clerk/public-records)).
  Clerk phone: 505-924-3650.
- **Account:** you create a guest account with an email and password, confirm the email, then sign in to
  follow the request, message staff, and download the records under "Documents"
  ([city job aid](https://www.cabq.gov/clerk/documents/job-aid-how-to-create-a-guest-profile-in-nextrequest.docx/@@download/file/Job%20Aid%20-%20How%20to%20Create%20a%20Guest%20Profile%20in%20NextRequest.docx)).
- **Public visibility (unverified):** the portal home page shows a public "Search" of requests
  ("26108 requests and counting") and gives no visibility notice. Assume your request text and the
  city's released documents may be visible to anyone. Put nothing private in it.
- **Fees:** from the [2026 fee schedule](https://www.cabq.gov/clerk/documents/public-facing-fee-schedule_ada_accessible.pdf)
  (signed 27 March 2026):
  - Paper: $1.00 per printed page.
  - 4 GB flash drive: $9.00.
  - The schedule lists **no charge for electronic delivery** (it does not mention it either way; the
    statute only allows "actual costs" of emailing). Ask for electronic delivery through the portal and
    the fee should be $0 **(unverified in practice)**.
  - Fees are invoiced and paid in advance, before copies are made.
  - Waiver: apply for a waiver of fees up to $25.00 per year with the "Fee Waiver Affidavit For IPRA
    Requests" form (Attachment A in the schedule). The Clerk decides.
- **Realistic turnaround:** the city's own [FY26 second/third quarter backlog report](https://www.cabq.gov/clerk/documents/fy26-secondthird-quarter-ipra-backlog-report_.pdf)
  says that over those two quarters:
  - 10,083 requests were received (about 45% from commercial requesters, 7,756 police-related);
  - 42% were closed within 20 days;
  - 1,719 requests were in the backlog, with 28 dedicated staff;
  - the city blames growth in police video requests (FY26 total: 21,705 requests, up 36%).
  
  Expect the 3-business-day acknowledgment and an extension notice. A small, specific, non-police
  document request should finish sooner than the average, but plan for weeks, not days.

## 3. Writing a request that gets filled fast

Rules of thumb:

1. **Name existing documents.** "The weekly Media Report PDF for week X" beats "all inspection info."
2. **Give identifiers.** Business name, address, permit number, inspection date.
3. **Keep scope narrow:** one topic and one date range per request. Split unrelated asks.
4. **Ask for electronic delivery** through the portal, in the file format the city already has.
5. **Do not give a reason.** It is not required and not helpful.
6. **Ask for the responsive records only,** not "all communications" or "all records related to."
7. **Invite narrowing:** say you will gladly narrow it if that speeds things up.

### Template

```
Subject: IPRA request: [short description]

Under the New Mexico Inspection of Public Records Act (NMSA 1978, 14-2-1 et seq.), I request
electronic copies of the following existing records:

1. [Document name, e.g. "Environmental Health food inspection Media Report PDF"]
   for [date range / week(s) ending ...].
2. [Complete inspection report(s) for [business name], [street address], permit [PT#######],
   inspection date(s) [dates].]
3. [All photographs taken by inspectors during the inspection(s) in item 2.]

Please deliver electronically through the portal, in the format the records already exist in.
If any part is exempt, please release the rest and cite the exemption for what you withhold.
If there will be a fee, please tell me the amount before copying. If this request seems too broad,
contact me and I will narrow it.

Name: [your name]
Mailing address: [address]
Telephone: [phone]
Email: [email]
```

Section 14-2-8(C) requires name, address and telephone number, so fill them in. Use a mailing
address and email you are comfortable having on a record that may be public.

### Worked example: Bamboo

```
Subject: IPRA request: food inspection reports and photographs, Bamboo, 7202 Menaul Blvd NE

Under the New Mexico Inspection of Public Records Act (NMSA 1978, 14-2-1 et seq.), I request
electronic copies of the following existing records for Bamboo, 7202 Menaul Blvd NE, Albuquerque,
permit PT0157444:

1. The complete food establishment inspection report for each inspection on June 8, 2026,
   June 16, 2026, and July 17, 2026.
2. All photographs taken by the inspector(s) during those three inspections.

Please deliver electronically through the portal, in the format the records already exist in.
If any part is exempt, please release the rest and cite the exemption for what you withhold.
If there will be a fee, please tell me the amount before copying. If this request seems too broad,
contact me and I will narrow it.

Name: [your name]
Mailing address: [address]
Telephone: [phone]
Email: [email]
```

One facility, three dates, two document types: narrow enough to be done quickly. If the city says photos
are held elsewhere (inspectors' devices, a separate system), expect that part to take longer. Keep the
two items in one request only if they come back together; if photos stall, file them separately so the
reports are not held up.

## 4. If they are late, deny, or redact

1. **No word after 3 business days:** that already breaks section 14-2-8(D). Reply in the portal,
   politely, asking for the written status the law requires.
2. **Day 15 with nothing:** the request "may be deemed denied." Send a short, polite follow-up in the
   portal, cite section 14-2-8(D), and ask for a date. A denial must be in writing within 15 days
   (section 14-2-11(B)).
3. **Redactions or denial:** ask which exemption applies to each redaction. A denial must name the people
   responsible. Narrowing the request often fixes it.
4. **Escalate.** Options, none needing a lawyer to start:
   - **Complaint to the NM Department of Justice** (the office that used to be the Attorney General's;
     it "reviews and issues determinations" on IPRA complaints per search results; the pages themselves
     blocked automated fetching, so confirm details **(unverified)**).
     Start at [IPRA page](https://nmdoj.gov/get-help/inspection-of-public-records-act/) or the
     [electronic complaint system](https://secure.nmdoj.gov/ECS/ECSIntakeTypeGroups). Reported caveats: filing
     does not guarantee an investigation; the complaint is itself a public record and may be sent to the
     agency; the Department does not represent you in court.
   - **Lawsuit:** section 14-2-12 lets a requester whose request was denied sue in district court
     without first exhausting any administrative step, and the court "shall award damages, costs and
     reasonable attorneys' fees" to a requester who wins. This is a last resort.
   - **New Mexico Foundation for Open Government** ([nmfog.org](https://www.nmfog.org/public-records))
     publishes guidance and may help **(not checked in detail)**.

Keep a log (date, what you sent, what came back). It is the evidence if you ever escalate.

## 5. MuckRock: "file it for me"

MuckRock is a nonprofit site that files and tracks public-records requests for you, handles follow-ups,
and posts the correspondence and released documents. It could be useful for a first request or for
getting a template.

What I found (all from search-result summaries; muckrock.com itself blocked automated access, so
**prices and rules below are unverified against the live site**, and the numbers may be stale):

- Account is free, no credit card.
- Filing starts at "$20 to file up to four requests" (from MuckRock's FOIA 101 page).
- Paid plans: help-center text lists Professional at $40/month and Organization at $100/month; an
  older (2019) announcement listed per-request prices of $3-$5. Do not rely on these. Check
  [accounts.muckrock.com](https://accounts.muckrock.com) before paying.
- Government fees are separate; if an agency requires payment, MuckRock adds 5% for transaction costs.
- **Requests are public by default.** Embargo is for paid plans only (up to 30 days at a time on the
  Professional plan; permanent on the Organization plan), per the help-center summary.
- Albuquerque requests already appear on MuckRock, but they still end up in the city's NextRequest portal.

Bottom line: for one narrow request to this city, filing directly on NextRequest is free and just as
good. MuckRock earns its cost when you want it done for you or want it on a public page.

## 6. Where the results go

Put every received PDF in this repo's archive, with the request number as the source and the date
received:

```
uv run nm-health archive --file <pdf> --source "IPRA <request number>" --seen-at <date received>
```

Then rebuild as usual. Keep the original request and the city's reply in the same place you keep your
log, not in `data/archive/`.
